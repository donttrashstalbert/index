Don't Trash St. Albert

Keep construction waste out of wetlands, natural areas, and our neighbourhoods.

Are you tired of seeing St. Albert’s natural areas getting trashed by construction waste? We pushed, and in 2026 the City finally strengthened its rules, but the garbage is still ending up in our wetlands, ponds, and forests, and bins are still left uncovered. Share what you’re seeing, get updates, and help push to get it fixed for good.

Where things stand in 2026

The rules changed. The mess didn’t. After more than a year of resident pressure, City Council strengthened St. Albert’s construction-waste rules in April 2026, and in July 2026 voted 6–1 to require builders to file a plan for managing construction debris. That is real progress, and it happened because residents spoke up.

But walk the ponds and forests today and the same problem is still there: garbage blowing into our wetlands and natural areas, and dumpsters left uncovered and overflowing. Residents keep filing new reports through the City’s Spruce It Up app. The job isn’t done until the mess actually stops, and until it’s builders and developers, not taxpayers, doing the cleanup.

Waste harms wildlife, water, and humans.

  • Ingestion: Wildlife mistake plastic, foam, and insulation for food. This can cause internal blockages or fill stomachs without providing nutrition, potentially leading to starvation or death.
  • Entanglement: Plastic sheeting, straps, and debris can wrap around animals' limbs, wings, and necks—restricting movement, causing injuries, and sometimes leading to drowning or death.
  • Microplastics: Plastics break down into microscopic fragments that spread through water and sediment. These particles enter food, drinking water, and accumulate in humans and wildlife.
  • Injuries: Nails, staples, glass, and sharp debris can cut feet, beaks, and mouths, leading to infections that may become fatal.
  • Habitat damage: Debris smothers aquatic plants and disrupts wetland habitat, reducing habitat quality for fish, birds, and insects.
  • Blockages: Animals lose access to food sources and shelter when debris piles create impassable barriers.
  • Community impacts: Beyond wildlife, plastic pollution creates real costs for residents—cleanup expenses, degraded public spaces, and potential health concerns for children and pets.

Dear Mayor and Councillors,

I’m writing because construction waste is still ending up in our wetlands, ponds, forests, and neighbourhoods. I keep seeing garbage in the same places, and dumpsters left uncovered and overflowing. This is not acceptable.

It also shouldn’t be the taxpayers’ problem to fix. It isn’t right that public works crews spend days hauling garbage out of our ponds, and that enforcement staff spend weeks chasing builders, to clean up what is a private mess. If a company profits from building here, it can clean up after itself.

Please put real accountability in place: expect developers to be proactive and hold their own builders to a clean site, and have the City collect security up front, a strong agreement it can actually draw on, so that when a site is a mess we can clean it up right away instead of waiting weeks. Our parks are not job-site dumpsters. A good neighbour cleans up their own mess.

Please keep pushing until this is actually resolved.
